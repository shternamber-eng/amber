Our Thinking

Philosophy

On Memory, Craft, and Permanence

On memory

A Yahrzeit board is not signage. A donor wall is not a list. These are places where names are held — where the act of remembering takes material form. We believe that the object which carries memory should itself be worth preserving. Not because it is expensive, but because it was made with the understanding that someone will stand before it, and feel something.

"We do not create objects. We create places of memory — for those who have passed, and for those who remain."

On craft

Every piece we make is made entirely by hand. There are no templates, no digital cutters, no mass-produced components painted to look handmade. The amber is selected piece by piece. The smalt is cut and placed by hand. The composition is drawn specifically for the space and the community that commissioned it.

This is slow. It cannot be otherwise. The slowness is not a limitation — it is the condition under which the work becomes what it is.

On material

We work with natural Baltic amber — prehistoric resin, millions of years in formation, harvested from Ukrainian deposits. No two pieces are identical. The colour, the inclusions, the way light moves through it — these are not manufactured qualities. They are the qualities of something genuinely natural.

We work with Murano smalt — glass made on the island of Murano by families who have made it for generations, using the same techniques that produced the mosaics of Ravenna and Constantinople. It is not a decorative material. It is a material with a history.

We choose these materials not because they are prestigious, but because they are right. They have weight. They will last.

"Amber is not decorative. It is ancient, warm, and irreplaceable. That is why we use it."

On commission

We do not sell from stock. We begin with a conversation — about the institution, the space, the people who will be honoured, and the atmosphere the piece should create. From that conversation, we develop a proposal. From the proposal, a work.

This means that every piece we make exists nowhere else in the world. It belongs to the space it was made for, and to the community that commissioned it.
